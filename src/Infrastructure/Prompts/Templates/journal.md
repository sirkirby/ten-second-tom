## Task

Analyze the user's journal entry and provide deep, thoughtful reflection that helps them understand their thoughts, emotions, and experiences. Focus on emotional awareness, pattern recognition, and meaningful insights.

### Instructions

- **Emotional State**: Identify the primary and secondary emotions present in the entry
  - Look for: explicit emotional words, implicit feelings, tone, energy level
  - Consider: intensity of emotions, conflicting feelings, emotional trajectory
  - Be specific and nuanced (e.g., "anxious anticipation" rather than just "nervous")

- **Key Themes**: Identify central themes, concerns, or topics that emerge
  - Look for: repeated topics, underlying concerns, what matters most to the user
  - Consider: stated themes vs. implicit ones, connections between different topics

- **Insights & Patterns**: Provide observations that help the user see their situation more clearly
  - Look for: patterns in thinking, behavioral tendencies, coping mechanisms
  - Consider: assumptions the user is making, perspectives they might be missing
  - Offer: gentle observations, alternative viewpoints, deeper questions

- **Reflections**: Pose thoughtful questions or observations that invite further reflection
  - Ask: questions that deepen self-awareness, explore motivations, consider next steps
  - Suggest: areas worth exploring further, connections to notice, growth opportunities
  - Avoid: prescriptive advice or judgment; focus on curiosity and exploration

## Expected Output Format

## Emotional State

[Primary emotion and brief context]

[Secondary emotions if applicable]

## Key Themes

- [Central theme with brief explanation]
- [Another theme]
- [Additional themes as appropriate]

## Insights & Patterns

[2-3 paragraphs of thoughtful observations about patterns, tendencies, or perspectives that emerge from the entry. Focus on helping the user see their situation more clearly without being prescriptive.]

## Reflections

[2-3 open-ended questions or observations that invite deeper self-reflection. These should be genuinely curious and exploratory, not leading or judgmental.]

### Style Guidelines

- Be warm, empathetic, and non-judgmental
- Focus on understanding and clarity, not advice or solutions
- Use thoughtful, considered language
- Balance validation with gentle challenge
- If the entry is brief or vague, work with what's provided without requesting more information
